## 1. The verdict

The brand is **partially reliant on promotional activity**: 43.0% of customers are discounted and 39.0% of projected CLV is discount-attached. But the data shows most of that is *habit, not stimulus* — the top 20% of customers (with low promo dependency) hold 59.5% of projected value. **Loyalty is being built, but the brand is also subsidising demand it does not need to.**

**What to do under this scenario:** (1) protect the top 20% — ring-fence them from discount-led messaging; (2) sequence the Promotional Sunset Plan from full-price-leaning tiers first, deferring the 1,326 discount-dependent customers to a final wave with a loyalty-programme soft-landing; (3) reallocate freed margin to ICP look-alike acquisition and organic-demand regions. See `reports/recommendations/` for the full playbook.

## 3. How loyalty was defined (and why this definition)

The dataset has no loyalty score, no churn label, and no timestamps, so loyalty had to be **constructed**, not declared. Two competing definitions were engineered and scored against five commercial criteria (revenue alignment, margin alignment, segment separation, robustness, explainability):

- **Definition A — Volume-led:** previous purchases + frequency + subscription.
- **Definition B — Margin-aware:** A + satisfaction + full-price ratio.

**Winner:** Definition B — Margin-aware. The framework validates loyalty against **`economic_value_proxy`** (a loyalty-FREE value metric) rather than against CLV, so the validation is not self-correlation. Full evaluation in [`reports/loyalty_framework.md`](loyalty_framework.md).

Every persona label in this project (Premium Loyalist, Promo-Dependent High-Value, At-Risk Premium, etc.) traces back to a stated combination of variables in [`src/business_logic/segmentation.py`](../src/business_logic/segmentation.py). There are no declarations without rules.

## 7. Caveats (the asterisks every number carries)

- **Subscription–discount confound:** every subscriber in the source data carries a discount. 'Subscribers are discount-dependent' is therefore a data artefact, not a behavioural finding — promo metrics are reported net of subscription where it matters.
- **Gender–discount confound:** no female customer in the source data received a discount. Geographic/segment discount rates partly reflect gender mix, not pure regional behaviour; treat promo-by-geography as directional and validate before acting.
- **No timestamps / churn labels:** loyalty, tenure, and CLV are engineered proxies built from `previous_purchases` (volume) and `frequency_of_purchases` (cadence). They rank customers reliably but are not audited financial figures.
